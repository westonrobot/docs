# Humanoid Maintenance

G1, H1-2, R1, H2 — see [Humanoids](/robot/intro#humanoids).

What is specific to humanoids. Routine checks, battery care, cleaning and storage apply to every platform and are on [Robot Maintenance](/guides/robot-maintenance).

:::caution Do not service the joints yourself

If you meet stiffness, play, unusual noise or motor faults in a joint, **[contact us](/support/before-you-contact-us)**
with your serial number rather than opening or lubricating it. There is no owner-serviceable lubrication schedule on these
platforms, and a joint opened incorrectly is an expensive repair.

There is no fixed lubrication schedule to follow — stiffness, play, noise or a
motor fault is the trigger, not an interval.

:::

What you *should* do:

- **Inspect foot pads** for wear and for embedded grit. Worn pads change traction and gait.
- **Check for play** by hand with the robot powered off and supported: a joint that has developed noticeable free movement is worth reporting early.
- **Keep the limb linkages and joint seals clean.** Grit works its way in.
- **Let overheated motors cool** rather than power-cycling repeatedly. A sudden limp into damping mode is thermal protection, not a fault.
- **Inspect after every fall**, including the shell, the wiring and the connectors, not just the joint that landed first. A humanoid falls further than a quadruped, so inspect the whole robot rather than the impact side.
- **Use the diagnostics tools** before raising a ticket. We publish a guide for the [G1](/guides/unitree/g1_diag_guide); for the H1-2, R1 and H2, [contact us](/support/before-you-contact-us) with your serial number and what you are seeing.
