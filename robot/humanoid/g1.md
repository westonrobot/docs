# G1

![Unitree G1 humanoid robot](../img/unitree/G1_robot.png)

The G1 is a humanoid robot standing 130 cm tall and weighing 35 kg, supplied with a LIVOX MID-360 LiDAR and an Intel RealSense D435i depth camera. It ships with two onboard computers — one running Unitree's own control stack, one for your code — and is developed against over a wired connection to its internal network.

## Identify your unit

Every support request starts with the serial number, because it identifies the exact hardware revision and the firmware the robot shipped with. There are two places to find it:

- **On the robot** — inside the **battery compartment**.
- **In the mobile app** — under `Device → Robot Settings`, which also shows the model designation.

The app is usually quicker, and the model designation matters: G1 ships in variants, and which one you have determines what hardware is fitted.

For other platforms, see [Identify your product](/support/identify-your-product).

## Getting started

Read [Operational Safety](/tutorial/operational-safety) before powering the robot for the first time. The G1 is a legged robot that can fall, and the guidance there on supporting it during early testing prevents the most common damage.

The first-run sequence is covered end to end in the **[G1 Development Guide](/tutorial/unitree/g1_dev_guide)**, the most detailed guide on this site. In outline:

1. **Understand the hardware** — joint layout, peripherals and electrical connectivity.
2. **Connect to the robot** — wire into its internal network and reach the development computer.
3. **Set up the software** — the SDK and the ROS 2 packages, and how Unitree's software is structured.
4. **Run an example** — a first motion program, to confirm the whole chain works.

If you only need the robot on the internet rather than a development setup, the [Internet Connection Guide](/tutorial/unitree/g1_internet_guide) is the shorter path.

### Onboard computers

G1 Edu carries two computers on the robot's internal `192.168.123.x` network. Knowing which is which saves a lot of confusion on first contact — **your code goes on the development computer**.

```mermaid
flowchart LR
    L["Your laptop<br/>192.168.123.222"] -->|Ethernet<br/>port 4 / 5| SW["Internal switch"]
    SW -->|ssh| DEV["<b>Development computer</b><br/>192.168.123.164<br/>unitree / 123"]
    SW --- CTL["Operation &amp; control<br/>192.168.123.161<br/>not user-accessible"]
    DEV -->|DDS| CTL
    style DEV fill:#0f6e78,stroke:#0f6e78,color:#fff
    style CTL fill:#e8e8e8,stroke:#999,color:#333
```

| Unit | IP | Credentials | What it is for |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Operation & control | `192.168.123.161` | — | Runs Unitree's locomotion and control stack. Not accessible to users. |
| User development | `192.168.123.164` | `unitree` / `123` | Where you install the SDK and run your own code. |

You reach the development computer over SSH and talk to the control computer from there over DDS — you do not log into the control computer directly. The [G1 Development Guide](/tutorial/unitree/g1_dev_guide) has fuller diagrams of the internal wiring and the two SDK approaches.

Development requires a **wired** connection. The development unit can join a WiFi network for *internet access* — to fetch packages, for example — but not for development itself. The [Internet Connection Guide](/tutorial/unitree/g1_internet_guide) covers both methods, including sharing your host machine's connection.

## Troubleshooting & FAQ

### Can the robot stand on its own?

From a lying position, the G1 can stand on its own. However, this mode is not recommended: it may damage the joints through excessive use, and the robot may fall on smooth floors. Have the robot stand from a seated or hanging position instead.

### Does the robot support wireless development?

No. The internal development PC can connect to WiFi for internet access — see the [Internet Connection Guide](/tutorial/unitree/g1_internet_guide) — but development itself requires a wired connection to the robot's internal network.
